orbit-launcher · design presentation v4 · the terminal truth

No polish. This is the product.

Every panel on this page is rendered the way the shipped app renders: an 80×26 grid of character cells, 10 frames per second, integer positions, brightness as a stepped glyph ramp with 24-bit colour. No blur, no easing, no subpixels. What looks good here ships; what doesn't, doesn't — regardless of how it looked in v2/v3, which are retained as the reference for a future Orbit web UI rebrand.

01 Splash — the orbital system, in cells

orbit-launcher

      

      

        
What survives the medium

        
    
          
  • The sphere holds up — opaque interior with a half-block
          (▀▄) limb, terminator shading, text inside. It is
          unmistakably a body, not a box.
    • 
          
  • Planets are single bright cells on tilted ellipses,
          40–150s periods. Watch one cross the top half: it genuinely
          vanishes behind the sphere and re-emerges on the other limb.
    • 
          
  • The starfield rotates rigidly — same two-layer clock as
          the design (360s/520s). At cell resolution the motion is nearly
          subliminal, which is the point.
    • 
          
  • Keybind footer is gone; version bottom-right — as
          decided.

The size it will usually be seen at

        
    
          
  • 80×26 is the floor, not the target. A maximized terminal
          on any modern display is 150–250 columns; this panel is the
          identical renderer at 160×44 — braille dot density doubles in each
          axis, so the ring resolves into a fine, even circle.
    • 
          
  • This is why the size tiers matter (handover 05): full
          tier gets this; the 80×26 floor gets a smaller, honest compact
          system; tiny windows drop the body entirely.

The immersive flow, honestly

        
    
          
  • This is mostly text, and text is what terminals do well —
          the console frame is box-drawing characters, the log is the real
          PTY stream, the spinner is the standard braille cycle. This screen
          loses almost nothing against its v3 mockup.
    • 
          
  • Stage bar is a run of ━ cells, filled in
          accent against faint — no percentage, only the elapsed clock top
          right.
    • 
          
  • Current stage in words under the bar, bottom-left.
