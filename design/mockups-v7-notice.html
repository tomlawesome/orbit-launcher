orbit-launcher · v7 — the development notice · #175

One new screen in the Install flow: profile → confirm → notice → mission console. Two things gate the install: the notice has been on screen to its end, and a 40-second timer has run out. Cell-truth renders, colours from internal/ui/style/style.go, every glyph one the app already draws. The sky and the countdown animate as the app would (10 fps, whole-cell steps); both stop under prefers-reduced-motion. Nothing here is approved yet.

Decisions, for review

  
Each is a proposal with its reasoning. None is approved.


  
1 · Showing there is more

  
The last visible prose row is drawn faint instead of muted, and the sentence is cut by the
    edge. Scrolled down, the top row goes faint too. That is the whole signal: no hint text, no corner
    glyph, no scroll track — the house has none, and a cut-off sentence is a thing everyone knows how
    to finish. A scroll track was considered and dropped: it adds a column of noise to say "a little
    more", and position does not matter for a text this short.


  
2 · Where the choices live, and the three states of accept

  
The timer row and the two choices are the last three rows of the document, so they scroll
    in with the text. Go back is live whenever it is on screen and takes the caret by default. The
    accept row has three looks: faint while either gate is open (not a choice yet),
    muted once both gates are passed (selectable), bright with the gold caret when
    selected. The caret never moves on its own: when accept opens up, the person moves to it with
    ↑. Nothing is pinned under the text: a second fixed region breaks the one-block
    grammar every screen shares, and a greyed-out button with no hint allowed is a mystery; a row
    that brightens next to a line that just finished filling explains itself.


  
3 · Every install

  
Every install, not once per machine. The notice is the install's own gate, like the
    confirm screen, and costs under a minute. A remembered "already agreed" needs a file somewhere
    that can be wrong — another user on the box, a copied image — and would make the launcher
    stateful for nothing. Update and repair never see it.


  
4 · Keys

  
↓ ↑ one row. PgDn space one screen; PgUp
    back. Home End top and bottom — End is a legitimate way to reach the end;
    blocking it would be pretend security, and the timer covers the reading time anyway. Wheel:
    three rows a notch, with mouse reporting switched on for this view only (Bubble Tea v2 sets it
    per view), so text selection on other screens is untouched. Arrows move the caret when the row
    they would land on is a live choice, and scroll the text otherwise — so ↑ from Go back
    goes to accept once it is open, and scrolls the prose back up while it is not. ↵
    acts on the caret only: before Go back is on screen it does nothing — it is the commit key
    everywhere else, and letting it page would turn a quick triple ↵ from the confirm
    screen into something it should not be. esc is Go back at any moment, timer or
    no timer: one step, to Ready to install, the same step the confirm screen's own esc
    takes.


  
5 · 80×26, and tall

  
At 80×26 the header (⟡, title, state, with a blank row above and below) takes six rows, two
    rows of sky stay clear beneath the block, and the viewport is the eighteen between; the document
    is twenty-one rows, so the timer and the two choices hang below the edge and three rows
    of scrolling reveal them. (At 80×24, a common default, the final paragraph hides too.) On a tall
    terminal nothing scrolls and nothing pretends to: the block centres as on every screen, Go back
    carries the caret, and the timer is the only gate. The scroll requirement is "the whole notice
    has been on screen", not "a scroll happened" — a forced scroll with nothing to scroll would be
    theatre.


  
6 · The timer

  
40 seconds. 144 words at 238 words a minute (Brysbaert 2019, average adult silent
    reading) is 36.3 s. Rounded up to the next ten: up because the average is the midpoint
    and half of readers are slower; to a round number because 0:36 looks computed and 0:40 looks
    chosen, and a countdown should look chosen. It starts when the screen appears, not on a key.
    Look: one row, in the block, between the prose and the choices — a thin line of
    ─ cells filling gold over the BorderSoft track (the mission console's
    own bar, at 50 cells one cell per 0.8 s, whole-cell steps) with the seconds left as a faint
    m:ss at its end (the console's clock format). No percentage. When it reaches 0:00
    the row goes blank — the gate is gone, so its object is gone — and the accept row brightens.
    Why in the block and not pinned: pinned, it would tick in the corner of the eye for the
    whole read, which is a cookie-banner timer; in the block it is seen by exactly the people it
    affects, the ones who reach the end before the time is up. On a tall terminal it is in view
    from the start, next to the thing it gates. It is not a motion beat, so
    ORBIT_LAUNCHER_NO_ANIMATION does not skip it — without the sky's tick chain it
    needs its own one-second tick.

  
Order does not matter. End first: the person waits by the choices with the line filling
    (scene f). Timer first: the line has already vanished when they scroll to the end, and the
    accept row is already muted — to a slow reader at 80×26 the timer never existed. Either way the
    caret is on Go back until they move it.


  

    
Proposal · choice wording

    
The draft labels are drawn as given. One alternative for the first, flagged only: "I've read
      this — install Orbit" says exactly what the two gates can prove. "Go back" stays.

  


  
In-grammar details

  
Amber ⟡, as the flow's two other cautionary screens use (stale volume, unavailable profile).
    "alpha" in the same amber — the one state colour, twice, as the splash pairs mark and status word.
    Prose left-aligned as one 56-column block, centred as a block: the sign-in screen's ratified
    grammar. No foot. The timer length should be a variable so tests can shorten it.
