orbit-launcher · v7 — the development notice · #175

One new screen in the Install flow: profile → confirm → notice → mission console. Two things gate the install: the prose has been on screen to its last line, and a 40-second timer has run out. The timer and the two choices are pinned in view from the first frame; only the prose scrolls. Cell-truth renders, colours from internal/ui/style/style.go, every glyph one the app already draws. The sky and the countdown animate as the app would (10 fps, whole-cell steps); both stop under prefers-reduced-motion. Nothing here is approved yet.

Decisions, for review

  
Each is a proposal with its reasoning. None is approved.


  
1 · Showing there is more

  
The last visible prose row is drawn faint instead of muted, and the sentence is cut by the
    edge. Scrolled down, the top row goes faint too. That is the whole signal: no hint text, no corner
    glyph, no scroll track — the house has none, and a cut-off sentence is a thing everyone knows how
    to finish. A scroll track was considered and dropped: it adds a column of noise to say "a little
    more", and position does not matter for a text this short. Where the cut lands depends on the
    terminal's height; the faint row is the cue that is always there, the hanging sentence a bonus
    (at 80×26 it is the first line of the "Never expose" paragraph).


  
2 · Where the choices live, and the three states of accept

  
The timer row and the two choices are pinned at the bottom of the frame, in view from the
    first frame at every size; only the prose between the header and them scrolls. Go back takes the
    caret from the start, so the way out is visible before anything is read. The accept row has
    three looks: faint while either gate is open (not a choice yet), muted once both
    are passed (selectable), bright with the gold caret when selected. The caret never moves
    on its own: when accept opens up, the person moves to it with ↑. A faint row that
    brightens beside a line that just finished filling explains itself without a hint. In the view
    this is still one content block (header lines, the visible prose rows, the bottom rows) handed to
    the same centring as every screen; the "pinning" is only that the first and last rows never
    scroll.


  
3 · Every install

  
Every install, not once per machine. The notice is the install's own gate, like the
    confirm screen, and costs under a minute. A remembered "already agreed" needs a file somewhere
    that can be wrong — another user on the box, a copied image — and would make the launcher
    stateful for nothing. Update and repair never see it.


  
4 · Keys

  
↓ ↑ one row. PgDn space one screen; PgUp
    back. Home End top and bottom — End is a legitimate way to reach the end;
    blocking it would be pretend security, and the timer covers the reading time anyway. Wheel:
    three rows a notch, with mouse reporting switched on for this view only (Bubble Tea v2 sets it
    per view), so text selection on other screens is untouched. ↓ ↑ scroll
    the prose until its last line has been on screen; from then on they move the caret between the
    two choices (↑ onto accept only once it is open), and PgUp/PgDn/Home/End/wheel keep
    scrolling the prose. ↵ acts on the caret, which starts on Go back — so a
    quick triple ↵ from the confirm screen lands back on Ready to install, never in an
    install. esc is Go back at any moment, timer or no timer: one step, to Ready to
    install, the same step the confirm screen's own esc takes.


  
5 · 80×26, and tall

  
At 80×26 the header (blank, ⟡, blank, title, state, blank) takes six rows and the bottom
    region (timer, accept, Go back, blank) four, leaving a sixteen-row viewport for eighteen rows of
    prose: the "Never expose" paragraph hangs below the edge and two rows of scrolling reveal
    it. "The end" is the prose's last line having been on screen. (At 80×24, a common default, the
    viewport is fourteen rows.) At the minimum size the block fills the frame top to bottom, so
    the 0.42 placement collapses to zero — there is no spare room, and none is needed. On a tall
    terminal nothing scrolls and nothing pretends to: the block centres as on every screen, Go back
    carries the caret, and the timer is the only gate. The scroll requirement is "the whole notice
    has been on screen", not "a scroll happened" — a forced scroll with nothing to scroll would be
    theatre.


  
6 · The timer

  
40 seconds. 144 words at 238 words a minute (Brysbaert 2019, average adult silent
    reading) is 36.3 s. Rounded up to the next ten: up because the average is the midpoint
    and half of readers are slower; to a round number because 0:36 looks computed and 0:40 looks
    chosen, and a countdown should look chosen. It starts when the screen appears, not on a key.
    Look: one row, in the block, between the prose and the choices — a thin line of
    ─ cells filling gold over the BorderSoft track (the mission console's
    own bar, at 50 cells one cell per 0.8 s, whole-cell steps) with the seconds left as a faint
    m:ss at its end (the console's clock format). No percentage. When it reaches 0:00
    the row goes blank — the gate is gone, so its object is gone — and, if the prose has also been
    read to its end, the accept row brightens. It sits directly under the prose and directly above
    the thing it gates, in view from the first frame at every size, faint enough not to pull the eye
    off the text. It is not a motion beat, so ORBIT_LAUNCHER_NO_ANIMATION does not skip
    it — without the sky's tick chain it needs its own one-second tick.

  
Order does not matter. End first: the person waits by the choices with the line filling
    (scene f). Timer first: the line vanishes while they are still reading; accept brightens the
    moment the last line scrolls in. Either way the caret is on Go back until they move it.


  
7 · Emphasis (owner, 2026-10-03)

  
Three phrases, wording untouched: "I'm not a professional software engineer", "use it at your own
    risk", "Never expose Orbit directly to the internet". Drawn bold, one ink step up from the row
    they sit in: on a normal (muted) row that is bold Text — the exact style every
    flow screen's title already uses — and on a faded row (the cut-off bottom line, the top line when
    scrolled) it is bold TextMuted, so the phrase steps down with its row and still
    stands one step above it. Whole cells, two existing tokens, no new colour: gold is the caret's
    and amber is state, and a warning phrase in either would read as a link or a fault. Under
    NO_COLOR the steps vanish and bold alone carries it, which is why bold is part of
    the rule and not a fallback. Scene a shows the hardest case: the hanging "Never expose" line is
    faint, and its phrase is still the brightest thing on that row.


  

    
Proposal · choice wording

    
The draft labels are drawn as given. One alternative for the first, flagged only: "I've read
      this — install Orbit" says exactly what the two gates can prove. "Go back" stays.

  


  
In-grammar details

  
Amber ⟡, as the flow's two other cautionary screens use (stale volume, unavailable profile).
    "alpha" in the same amber — the one state colour, twice, as the splash pairs mark and status word.
    Prose left-aligned as one 56-column block, centred as a block: the sign-in screen's ratified
    grammar. Every line of the view, header included, is padded to the block's width plus a two-cell
    gutter each side (60 cells), so compositeScene keeps the sky — stars and planets —
    out of that rectangle, as it does under any padded line today. No foot. The timer length should
    be a variable so tests can shorten it.


  
Changed since the first pass

  
· Timer and choices were the document's tail and started below the fold at 80×26; now they are
    pinned and in view from the first frame, and only the prose scrolls.

    · Planets sat flush against the text; every line is now padded to a 60-cell rectangle the sky
    never enters.

    · The renderer clipped wide glyphs, so the em dash and ● drew as a short dash and a half disc;
    cells no longer clip. The labels use the em dash as approved.

    · Arrow keys: a simpler rule now that the choices do not scroll (see 4).

    · Third pass: the owner's three phrases are emphasised (see 7), in every scene including the
    faded rows.
