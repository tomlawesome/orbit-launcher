One new screen in the Install flow: profile → confirm → notice → mission console. Three things gate the install: the prose has been on screen to its last line, a 70-second timer has run out, and the person has typed I've read this and I understand. The timer, the typed field and Go back are pinned in view from the first frame; only the prose scrolls. Copy is the owner's approved final text, verbatim. Cell-truth renders, colours from internal/ui/style/style.go, every glyph one the app already draws. The sky and the countdown animate as the app would (10 fps, whole-cell steps); both stop under prefers-reduced-motion. Nothing else here is approved yet.

Decisions, for review

  
Each is a proposal with its reasoning. The copy, the phrase and the prompt label are approved;
    the rest is not.


  
1 · Showing there is more

  
The last visible prose row is drawn faint instead of muted, and the sentence is cut by the
    edge. Scrolled down, the top row goes faint too. That is the whole signal: no hint text, no corner
    glyph, no scroll track — the house has none, and a cut-off sentence is a thing everyone knows how
    to finish. A scroll track was considered and dropped: it adds a column of noise to say "a little
    more", and position does not matter for a text this short. One rule keeps the cue honest at every
    height: a paragraph gap is never the viewport's last row while more follows — the next
    line is pulled up into its place — so the bottom row is always a line of text, faint, and usually
    cut mid-sentence. Without it, the 80×26 first view of this copy would have ended on a blank row
    after "up to you.", which reads as an ending.


  
2 · The bottom region and its states

  
Pinned at the bottom of the frame, in view from the first frame at every size: the timer row,
    the prompt, the field, a feedback row (usually blank: the gap) and Go back. Only the prose
    between the header and them scrolls. Go back takes the caret from the start, so the way out is
    visible before anything is read. The field has two states: asleep while either gate is
    open (prompt faint, field row empty, letters ignored) and awake once both are passed
    (prompt brightens, the gold caret and cursor appear). Nothing moves on its own when it wakes:
    the caret stays on Go back until the person types. In the view this is still one content block
    handed to the same centring as every screen; the "pinning" is only that the first and last rows
    never scroll.


  
3 · Every install

  
Every install, not once per machine. The notice is the install's own gate, like the
    confirm screen, and costs about a minute. A remembered "already agreed" needs a file somewhere
    that can be wrong — another user on the box, a copied image — and would make the launcher
    stateful for nothing. Update and repair never see it.


  
4 · Keys

  
PgDn space-before-the-field-wakes, PgUp, Home
    End and the wheel (three rows a notch, mouse reporting on for this view only) always
    scroll the prose; a page is the viewport less one row, so the last line read is the first line
    seen. ↓ ↑ scroll it too, except that ↓ from the awake field
    steps the caret onto Go back, and ↑ from Go back steps it into the awake field — the
    caret walks the page: prose, then field, then Go back. Letters and space go to the
    field whenever it is awake, wherever the caret is; typing is what claims it. The field has no
    cursor movement, so Home End stay with the prose, and backspace
    trims — the configure screen's field works the same way. ↵ on Go back goes
    back; in the field it submits (see 8); a quick triple ↵ from the confirm screen lands
    back on Ready to install, never in an install. esc is Go back at any moment:
    one step, to Ready to install, the same step the confirm screen's own esc takes.


  
5 · 80×26, and tall

  
The approved copy wraps to thirty-eight rows at the 56-column measure (nine paragraphs,
    eight gaps). At 80×26 the header (blank, ⟡, blank, title, state, blank) takes six rows and the
    bottom region (timer, prompt, field, feedback, Go back, blank) six, leaving a fourteen-row
    viewport: twenty-four rows hang below the edge — two PgDn, or one
    End, to reach the last line. "The end" is the prose's last line having been on screen.
    (At 80×24, a common default, the viewport is twelve rows.) At the minimum size the block fills
    the frame top to bottom, so the 0.42 placement collapses to zero — there is no spare room, and
    none is needed. At 120×50 the viewport is thirty-eight rows: it fits exactly, with no row
    to spare, so the block fills that frame too and nothing scrolls; at 120×49 the last line hangs
    faint below the edge and one ↓ shows it — the same screen, no special case. The scroll
    requirement is "the whole notice has been on screen", not "a scroll happened".


  
6 · The timer

  
70 seconds. The approved copy is 257 words (title and state line included, since they
    are read too); at 238 words a minute (Brysbaert 2019, average adult silent reading) that is
    64.8 s. Rounded up to the next ten, as before: up because the average is the midpoint and
    half of readers are slower; to a round number because 1:05 looks computed and 1:10 looks chosen.
    It starts when the screen appears, not on a key. Look: one row, directly under the prose —
    a thin line of ─ cells filling gold over the BorderSoft track (the
    mission console's own bar; 50 cells, so one cell every 1.4 s, whole-cell steps) with the seconds
    left as a faint m:ss at its end (the console's clock format), counting from 1:10.
    No percentage. When it reaches 0:00 the row goes blank — the gate is gone, so its object is gone.
    It is not a motion beat, so ORBIT_LAUNCHER_NO_ANIMATION does not skip it — without
    the sky's tick chain it needs its own one-second tick. Order does not matter: end first,
    the person waits by the sleeping field with the line filling (scene f); timer first, the line
    vanishes while they are still reading and the field wakes the moment the last line scrolls in.


  
7 · Emphasis (owner, 2026-10-03)

  
Three phrases, wording untouched: "I'm not a professional software engineer", "use it at your own
    risk", "Never expose Orbit directly to the internet". Drawn bold, one ink step up from the row
    they sit in: bold Text on a normal (muted) row — the style every flow screen's
    title already uses — and bold TextMuted on a faded row, so the phrase steps down
    with its row and still stands one step above it. Whole cells, two existing tokens, no new colour:
    gold is the caret's and amber is state. Under NO_COLOR the steps vanish and bold
    alone carries it, which is why bold is part of the rule and not a fallback. The new paragraphs
    carry no emphasis; the owner chose three phrases and three is what makes them stand out.


  
8 · The typed confirmation (owner, 2026-10-03)

  
Look: the configure screen's field, unchanged — gold ▸, the typed text in
    Text, a gold ▏ cursor, and a refusal in amber DegradedText
    on the row beneath, exactly as viewConfigCollect draws a reason. Above it one prompt
    row, To install, type in muted ink and the phrase itself in bright ink, so the thing
    to type is unmistakable without quotes. That is the question, not a key hint, so the house rule
    allows it. Asleep until both gates pass, then awake: typing takes five seconds against a
    seventy-second wait, a field that accepts letters it cannot yet submit invites a puzzled wait, and
    waking is the "something changed" the person is watching for. What matches (owner rulings,
    2026-10-03: capitals and apostrophes don't matter, "I have" equals "I've", words do matter): both
    sides are compared after lower-casing, removing every apostrophe-like mark (' ’ ‘ ´ `),
    collapsing runs of spaces to one, trimming the ends and dropping one trailing full stop; then they
    must be identical, except that after normalising, a leading "i have" counts as "ive". So
    "ive read this and i understand", "I'VE READ THIS AND I UNDERSTAND", "I have read this and I
    understand" and a curly ’ all pass; a missing or different word — "I read this and I
    understand", "I've read it" — fails. ↵ on an empty field does nothing; on a
    partial or wrong phrase it shows That isn't the phrase yet — type it exactly as shown in
    amber, keeps the typed text for fixing, and the next keystroke clears the message. No attempt
    counter: nothing is at stake in retrying. Go back stays a visible row: Esc needs no hint,
    but a visible way out is what every flow screen offers, and a text field with no neighbour would
    leave a stuck person with nothing to try but keys they cannot be told about.


  
In-grammar details

  
Amber ⟡, as the flow's two other cautionary screens use (stale volume, unavailable profile).
    "alpha" in the same amber — the one state colour, twice, as the splash pairs mark and status word.
    Prose left-aligned as one 56-column block, centred as a block: the sign-in screen's ratified
    grammar; the security address github.com/tomlawesome/orbit/security is 37 cells and
    never breaks across rows at that measure. Every line of the view, header included, is padded to
    the block's width plus a two-cell gutter each side (60 cells), so compositeScene
    keeps the sky — stars and planets — out of that rectangle, as it does under any padded line
    today. No foot. The timer length should be a variable so tests can shorten it.
