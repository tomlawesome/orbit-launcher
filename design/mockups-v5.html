orbit-launcher · design presentation v5 · the hybrid, in cells

The shipped splash, plus what survived.

Every panel renders under real terminal constraints — cell grid, 10fps, integer positions, glyph ramps. The base is the splash that ships today, untouched in layout. Two additions from the design work, both proven in cells: the starfield now rotates rigidly with two-layer parallax (instead of random per-star drift), and small planetary systems live out in the field — a binary pair circling each other, and a planet with a moon. No central ring, no corona.

01 Splash — cycling through dormant · alive · degraded

orbit-launcher

      

      

        
Watch it for thirty seconds

        
    
          
  • Layout is the shipped splash, exactly — ⟡ mark, wordmark,
          tagline, five menu items with the group gap, keybind hint at the
          bottom. Nothing moved.
    • 
          
  • The starfield is now a rigid, rotating body — one slow
          revolution (~6 min near layer, ~8.5 min far layer). Stars twinkle
          in place; no star ever wanders on its own. At cell resolution the
          rotation is nearly subliminal — you sense it more than see it.
    • 
          
  • Two small systems live in the field: a binary pair
          (ice + rose) circling each other upper-right, and a pale planet
          with an ember moon lower-left. Single-cell bodies, slow periods,
          placed clear of the text column. Company, not spectacle.
    • 
          
  • This panel cycles the three states every ten seconds so
          you can see the surviving vocabulary: dormant (accent mark,
          silence), alive (green mark, status line bottom-left, caret
          preselects Update), degraded (amber mark, status line, caret
          preselects Repair). The real app computes its state once at
          startup — no cycling.
    • 
          
  • Version bottom-right, always.
