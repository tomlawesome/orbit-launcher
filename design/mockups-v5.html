orbit-launcher · design presentation v5 · the hybrid, in cells

The shipped splash, plus what survived.

Every panel renders under real terminal constraints — cell grid, 10fps, integer positions, glyph ramps. Changes this revision: the starfield moves as one locked body (every star shifts in the same instant), the wordmark is a large half-block pixel ORBIT, the tagline is replaced by the deployment's FQDN with the status word beneath it, and the success screen's URL sits where the splash puts its FQDN.

01 Splash — cycling through dormant · alive · degraded

orbit-launcher

      

      

        
Watch it for thirty seconds

        
    
          
  • The sky moves as one. The rotation is quantized: the whole
          field re-renders in a single synchronized step every 2.5s (near
          layer steps a little further than the far layer — parallax kept).
          Between steps nothing moves but twinkle. No star ever shifts on
          its own; they are locked in their permanence, and it's your head
          that turned.
    • 
          
  • ORBIT is a proper wordmark now — a half-block pixel
          font (5×6 pixel letters rendered with ▀▄█, two pixel
          rows per cell), the standard TUI big-text technique. Three cell
          rows tall, genuinely bold. The ⟡ mark sits above it and carries
          the state colour.
    • 
          
  • The tagline is gone. In its place: the deployment's
          FQDN in the same quiet grey, with the status word — alive
          (green) or degraded (amber) — beneath it. With no
          deployment, just dormant (faint) sits under the wordmark.
          No bottom-left status line anymore; identity lives in one place.
    • 
          
  • Two small systems in the field: the binary pair
          (ice + rose) upper-right, the pale planet with its ember moon
          lower-left. Single-cell bodies, clear of the text column.
    • 
          
  • This panel cycles the three states every ten seconds
          (demo only — the real app computes state once at startup). The
          caret preselects Install / Update / Repair to match.

03 Success — URL in the splash's identity slot

    

      

        
orbit-launcher — ready

        

      

      

        
Same identity block as the splash

        
    
          
  • The URL sits exactly where the splash puts its FQDN —
          directly under the wordmark, with alive beneath it. Arriving
          here and returning to the splash later shows the same block in the
          same place; the app has one idea of identity.
    • 
          
  • URL keeps the accent colour here — it's the thing you came
          for — and is OSC 52 copyable.
    • 
          
  • Stacked actions in splash grammar: Get into Orbit / Terminal /
          Menu. Footer: "Orbit achieved in 3m 42s" left, version right.
    • 
          
  • The binary pair is still out there — success is the same
          universe as the splash, just a better day in it.
